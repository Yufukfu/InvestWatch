# Design

## Context

`analytics/` on `main` has a pure `analyze(doc, *, registry, catalogue, now, new_id)` that routes `firm_own` documents to M2 (rules R1-R3) and drops everything else with `module_not_implemented` (`modules/router.py`, skeleton design decision 5). The unmerged `analytics-worker` wraps it with the Redis stream consumer, the Postgres upsert on `(document_id, module)` and the `iw:events` publisher, and it adds `AdvertiserRef` to `Finding` (worker commit `b35348b`). Its publisher already applies the per-module thresholds M1 0.6 / M3 0.7 (ADR 0305), so M1/M3 findings flow end to end once the pipeline returns them. The upsert per `(document_id, module)` also allows one document to carry an M1 and an M3 finding, and it is the "mechanizm okresowej aktualizacji" the challenge asks for: a re-scan of the same URL updates the finding instead of duplicating it.

The challenge criteria come from `doc/Opis Wyzwania HackNation UKNF 19.11.pdf` (pp. 1-4) and its annexes, as summarized for this change by the coordinating session on Damian's request:
- M2 covers only "zarejestrowane w Polsce firmy inwestycyjne" (their own marketing).
- M1 covers ads of registered firms published "przez podmioty trzecie lub osoby prywatne", including Polish-language ads of foreign firms operating cross-border. The required output is an inventory: "dane firmy inwestycyjnej, dane podmiotu reklamującego, treść reklamy oraz… URL wraz z mechanizmem okresowej aktualizacji".
- M3 looks for offers "mogących wskazywać" on investment advice by entities outside the registry. Art. 76 of the Act on Trading in Financial Instruments (annex 3) defines advice as a recommendation based on the client's situation and concerning one or more financial instruments; the annex summarizes it as "rekomendacje nabywania, zbywania albo trzymania instrumentów finansowych". Instruments are those listed in Art. 2-3; plain crypto-assets (BTC, ETH) are not listed, crypto CFDs, futures and options are (Art. 2 lit. h/i). M3 output: "dane podmiotu, treść oferty oraz lokalizację (URL)".
- p. 2: an aggregator merges modules 1-3 into unified objects, filterable by date, source, risk level and module. The `Finding` v1 contract already covers this (`module`, `platform`, `detected_at`, `score` → `risk_level` in the API).

The signals, the score shape and the M3 gate come from `.docs/analytics-plan.md` §4 and §6, adjusted to those criteria. The contract is `Finding` v1 (`.docs/integration-plan.md` §4): `rule_id` accepts `S1.x`/`S3.x`, and `advertiser` has `name`, `handle`, `profile_url`, `website`, `in_registry` and `on_warning_list`. The mocks in `contracts/mocks/` show the intended M1/M3 output for six documents. They are illustrative, not a gold set: some of their evidence is not in the document (`Blog finansowy`), some results come from an LLM, and one (`8721cb75…` as M1 without a firm) contradicts the routing below. They are also the test double behind `contracts/mock-server/` and the `web/` mock mode (Damian's rule), so this change only reads them: tests load `documents.json` and `findings.json` read-only, nothing regenerates or edits them, and every difference between M1/M3 output and the mock findings goes into `follow-ups.md` with a disposition. Differences known at proposal time: `8721cb75…` (no finding vs an M1 finding), the advertiser name and S3.1 evidence of `72acbb3b…` (page host vs `Blog finansowy`), S1.5/S3.3/S3.4 `uncertain` rules-only vs the mocks' LLM results, the extra S1.7/S3.6 results, and the scores.

The sibling stage-2 change `analytics-llm-p0` builds the LLM client (OpenAI-compatible, ADR 0304), the prompt, the Redis cache and the M2 P0 rules. Its design (§1 "the engine defers, the judge decides", §2 "the seam for `analytics-m1-m3`") defines the seam, and this change follows it (decision 7) instead of defining a second one.

## Goals / Non-Goals

**Goals:**
- Route by who publishes and which registered firm is concerned, as the challenge defines the modules, not by `source_kind` alone.
- Rules-only M1 and M3 that produce contract-valid, explainable findings for the six mock documents, without an LLM.
- M1 and M3 on the shared rule engine (YAML catalogues, evidence safety net), so the LLM seam of `analytics-llm-p0` applies to them without new merge code.
- The LLM fills S1.4 (adds only), S1.5, S3.3 and S3.4 later, with a defined rules-only degradation.
- Only small, additive edits to `rules/engine.py`, and none to `rules/m2.py` or `m2_rules.yaml`, which `analytics-llm-p0` changes.

**Non-Goals:**
- The LLM client, prompt text, cache and response parsing (`analytics-llm-p0`).
- The topic gate, embeddings, near-duplicates (plan §2 steps 1-2).
- The KNF public warnings list (S3.7), the real KNF loader, inflected firm names.
- Weight calibration on a gold set (plan §8). The weights here are hand-tuned starting values.
- The aggregator and its filters (API and web; the contract already carries the fields).

## Decisions

1. **Publisher identity.** Before routing, analytics decides whether the publisher is a registry firm: the author's `profile_url` host or the page host is one of the firm's `website_domains` (equal or subdomain; social platform hosts never count), or the normalized author handle (lowercase, `@` and non-alphanumerics removed) equals a normalized name, core name or alias of the firm. This works on every platform, including Facebook, Instagram and TikTok. The challenge says M1 is about third parties and private persons, so a firm's own account is M2 whatever `source_kind` the scraper set.

2. **Routing.** One document can produce up to two findings, at most one per module:
   - Publisher is a registry firm → **M2** for that firm (also for a `third_party`/`unknown` document, e.g. the firm's Instagram account).
   - `firm_own` with a registry match by text or page host → **M2**, unchanged (the source configuration says it is the firm's page).
   - `firm_own` with no registry match → **M3** if advisory signals are present, otherwise no finding (`no_signals`). Never M2: M2 is only for registered firms. This replaces skeleton design decision 5, which sent such pages to M2 with R1 `fail`.
   - `third_party`/`unknown`, publisher not a firm, a registry firm mentioned (text or link host, decision 3) → **M1** for that firm and the publisher as advertiser, unless a foreign firm's ad does not target Poland (decision 5). Additionally **M3** against the publisher when the advisory offer comes from the publisher (decision 9).
   - `third_party`/`unknown`, publisher not a firm, no registry firm → **M3** if advisory signals are present, otherwise no finding (`no_signals`).
   "Advisory signals present" means at least one of S3.2, S3.3, S3.4, S3.5 or S3.8 is `fail` or `uncertain`. S3.1 and S3.6 alone describe almost every Polish page and are not evidence of an offer. Alternative: route by `source_kind` and the registry match only (the first draft). Rejected because it sent firm accounts to M1 and unregistered "own" pages to M2, against the module definitions.

3. **Link hosts count as a registry match for third-party content.** `Registry.match(text, url)` is unchanged for M2. For `third_party`/`unknown`, when the text and page host match nothing, the hosts of `links[].href` are compared with firm domains (same subdomain rule as the page host), with confidence 0.9 and the matching href as the quote. A finfluencer post that links to `eurotrade-markets.example.com/…?ref=…` advertises that firm even if the caption never names it. A malformed href is skipped. A fuzzy-only match still routes to M1, but S1.1 is then `uncertain` (no quote), which addresses skeleton follow-up #11 for M1.

4. **Signal results: `fail` means the signal is present.** This follows the mocks (`S1.3 #współpraca` is `fail`, its absence is `pass`). `pass` means absent, `uncertain` means a rule found a candidate that only the LLM can confirm, and `not_applicable` means the inputs to judge it are missing (for example S1.6 on a social post with no author). For M1 the signals are attributes of an inventory entry, not accusations: the dashboard shows them as "what makes this an ad". S1.5 has no rule part: without a verdict it is `uncertain` with null evidence and the explanation "wymaga modelu językowego", because the `analytics-llm-p0` engine asks the LLM only about results that exist.

5. **M1 is an inventory with a confidence score.** The M1 score means "how certain this is a third-party ad of registered firm X". A partnership label (S1.3) raises that certainty strongly; a missing label does not lower it (absence contributes 0). S1.7 "Kierowanie do odbiorców w Polsce" uses the same detection as S3.6 (PLN/zł amount, `+48`, `.pl` page host, else the `pl` language guess). It is a filter only for foreign firms: when the matched firm's `registry_type` is `foreign_branch` or `foreign_no_branch` and S1.7 is `pass` (no Polish marker), the candidate produces no finding, with the log reason `not_targeting_poland`, because the challenge scopes foreign firms to "polskojęzyczne reklamy zagranicznych firm" (cross-border ads aimed at Polish readers). A firm registered in Poland (`brokerage_house`, `brokerage_bank`) is in M1 scope whatever the language; there S1.7 is only recorded as an attribute. When S1.7 is `fail` it adds a small weight.

6. **The shared engine, with M1/M3 catalogues.** Signals are `engine.Rule` subclasses registered with `@register`, and they run through `engine.run_rules`, as `analytics-llm-p0` asks (its design §2). `rules/m1_signals.yaml` and `rules/m3_signals.yaml` use the M2 entry format plus an entry `weight` (number) and a root `bias` (number), read only for M1/M3. The loader takes the module: ids must be `R…` for M2, `S1.<n>` for M1 and `S3.<n>` for M3. The signal parameter keys (decision 14) are added to `KNOWN_PARAMS`, so typos still fail at load. Names come from `generate_mocks.py` where the mocks have them. A separate signal engine with its own judge protocol was the first draft; it was dropped once the sibling published its seam, because two protocols would need two merge paths. The cost is that both stage-2 branches edit `rules/engine.py` (additive), and the second to land rebases.

   **Evidence may quote a named document field.** Several signals are about metadata: the author handle (S1.6, S3.1), a link href (S1.1 by link, S1.2), the page URL (S3.6/S1.7 `.pl`). `Rule` gains `evidence_fields(ctx) -> list[str]`, `[doc.text]` by default; M1/M3 signal classes that quote metadata override it to add `author.name`, `author.handle`, each `links[].href` and `url`. The engine's safety net checks the quote against those fields; a quote found in none becomes `uncertain` with null evidence (the `analytics/CLAUDE.md` rule). The explanation says where a metadata quote was found ("w linku", "autor", "adres strony"). The contract `evidence` stays a plain string. LLM verdicts are verified against `text` only, by the judge.

7. **LLM seam: the `analytics-llm-p0` one.** `analytics-llm-p0` owns the `LlmJudge` protocol, `SemanticCheck`, `LlmVerdict` and `JudgeOutcome` in `llm/judge.py`, the YAML `llm: {question, when}` block, `run_rules(catalogue, ctx, judge=None, module=…, module_context=…)`, quote verification, chunking, cache, breaker and the `judge` parameter of `analyze`. This change owns which signals carry an `llm:` block, their Polish questions, the module contexts (`M1_CONTEXT` with the M1 definition; `M3_CONTEXT` with Art. 76 and the Art. 2-3 instrument list from annex 3) and the rules-only fallbacks:
   - No `llm:` block (S1.1, S1.2, S1.3, S1.6, S1.7, S3.1, S3.2, S3.5, S3.6, S3.8): rules only; a deterministic result is never overruled.
   - S1.4, `when: always`, with an `ask_llm` override that asks only when the rules result is `pass`: the LLM can add an implicit call to action, never remove a dictionary hit.
   - S1.5, S3.3, S3.4, `when: always`: rules-only, S1.5 is `uncertain` with null evidence; S3.3/S3.4 are `uncertain` quoting the rule candidate, or `pass` with no candidate. A verified verdict replaces the result (`hybrid` when the rule had evidence, else `llm`).
   - No judge, or no verdict (down, breaker open, budget, invalid output): the rules-only results stand and `model.llm = null`; otherwise `model.llm` comes from `JudgeOutcome.model`, as for M2.
   Landing order: groups 1-5 need nothing from `analytics-llm-p0`; before it lands, `llm:` blocks are ignored by the skeleton loader (it reads only known entry keys). Group 6 wires the judge and starts only after `analytics-llm-p0` is merged.

8. **M3 scoring follows Art. 76.** `score = σ(bias + Σ wᵢ·xᵢ)` (decision 10) with the gate: when S3.4 (recommendation to buy, sell or hold instruments) is neither `fail` nor `uncertain`, the score is `min(score, 0.4)` and the summary says so. So personalization plus instruments is strongest, an instrument recommendation alone is strong (above the 0.7 threshold once confirmed), personalization without instruments is weak (capped), and general education is capped low or has no finding. An `uncertain` S3.4 lifts the gate, so a rules-only page like `007bbcee…` can still reach the threshold; the finding then shows "do weryfikacji". This replaces plan §6's "no S3.4 and no S3.3".

9. **M1 and M3 on the same document.** When a third-party document is routed to M1 and S3.3 or S3.4 is `fail` or `uncertain`, analytics also emits an M3 finding against the publisher, if the offer is the publisher's: the sentence holding the S3.3/S3.4 evidence does not name the matched registry firm (name, core or alias). A finfluencer's own buy/sell calls or a paid "portfel szyty na miarę" are the publisher's; "Alfa Inwestycje DM oferuje doradztwo" is the firm's offer, which is the firm's business and gives no M3. The M3 finding has `firm: null` and the publisher as `advertiser`. This sentence-level attribution is a rules-only heuristic; the LLM may refine it later through the S3.3/S3.4 questions, which ask whose offer it is.

10. **Scores and starting weights.** `score = σ(bias + Σ wᵢ·xᵢ)` with `x = 1` for `fail`, `0.5` for `uncertain`, `0` otherwise, rounded to 3 decimals. Starting values, tuned so the scenarios hold rules-only and to be recalibrated on the gold set:
    - M1: bias −3.5; S1.1 0.5, S1.2 2.5, S1.3 2.0, S1.4 1.0, S1.5 2.0, S1.6 1.0, S1.7 0.5.
    - M3: bias −3.0; S3.1 0.5, S3.2 1.0, S3.3 1.5, S3.4 3.0, S3.5 1.0, S3.6 0.5, S3.8 0.5.
    Worked rules-only results: `3c41441c…` M1 ≈ 0.95; `182e5a42…` M1 ≈ 0.82 (labeled cooperation: certainly an ad); a bare firm mention by a Polish third party ≈ 0.38; `007bbcee…` M3 ≈ 0.90; `cdbf5306…` M3 ≈ 0.62 (≈ 0.88 once S3.4 is confirmed); S3.4 confirmed alone (with S3.1, S3.6) ≈ 0.73; S3.3 confirmed alone ≤ 0.4 (gate); `72acbb3b…` M3 ≈ 0.27; crypto-only buy calls ≈ 0.18. Tests assert scenario orderings and threshold sides, not these exact numbers.

11. **Crypto-assets (S3.8).** Plain crypto-assets (`bitcoin`, `BTC`, `ethereum`, `ETH`, `kryptowaluty`, `altcoiny`) are not instruments under Art. 2, so they never make S3.4 a candidate. Crypto CFDs, futures and options are instruments: `CFD`, `kontrakty terminowe`, `futures`, `opcje` match the S3.4 instrument list whatever the underlying. A buy/sell call on plain crypto-assets only is reported as S3.8 "Rekomendacje kryptoaktywów (poza art. 2)", severity low, weight 0.5, with an explanation that it is outside Art. 2. It counts as an advisory signal (the document gets an M3 finding), but it does not lift the gate, so such a finding stays at most 0.4. Reason: dropping it would hide crypto signal groups that analysts may still want to forward under MiCA, while the cap keeps them out of `finding.high_risk`.

12. **Advertiser.** `name` is `author.name`, else `author.handle`, else the page host. `handle` and `profile_url` come from `author`. `website` is `scheme://host/` of the page URL when the document has no author. A `profile_url` that is not an absolute http(s) URL becomes `null` instead of failing the finding. Strings are trimmed and cut to 200 characters (worker follow-up #8). `in_registry` is the publisher identity of decision 1 (always false on M1/M3 findings, since a firm publisher routes to M2). `on_warning_list` is `null` while S3.7 is not implemented. M1 findings carry `firm` from the match; M3 findings carry `firm: null`. M2 findings keep `advertiser: null`.

13. **Texts.** Titles and summaries are Polish and built from the results only. M1: `"{advertiser} reklamuje {firm}"` plus the strongest present signal names; the summary is an inventory line (firm, advertiser, label present or not). M3: `"Podmiot spoza rejestru {advertiser}: …"`. Summaries list present and `uncertain` signals, name S1.5 as not checked when it has no verdict, say when the gate capped the score or that S3.8 is outside Art. 2, and say "tryb regułowy (bez modelu językowego)" when `model.llm` is null. `content_excerpt` follows M2 (normalized text, at most 500 characters).

14. **Signal dictionaries (starting set, in YAML).** S1.2: link query keys `ref`, `aff`, `affiliate`, `partner`, `promo`, `coupon`, `referral` and text phrases `kod polecający`, `kod promocyjny`, `z moim linkiem`, `z kodem`; `utm_` alone gives `uncertain`. S1.3: `#reklama`, `#ad`, `#współpraca`, `#sponsored`, `współpraca reklamowa`, `płatna współpraca`, `materiał sponsorowany`, `sponsorowany`, `partner`, plus a link with `rel` containing `sponsored`, with R2's same-line negation rule. S1.4: imperatives such as `otwórz konto`, `załóż konto`, `zarejestruj się`, `odbierz bonus`, `link w bio`, `kliknij`, `dołącz`, `skorzystaj z kodu`. S3.2: `family office`, `wealth management`, `doradztwo inwestycyjne`, `doradca inwestycyjny`, `zarządzanie portfelem`, `indywidualna strategia`. S3.3 candidates: `dopasujemy … do Twojej sytuacji`, `konsultacja 1:1`, `ankieta inwestycyjna`/`profil inwestycyjny` (not a bare `ankieta`, which is usually a broker's MiFID questionnaire), `indywidualnie dobierzemy`, `szyty na miarę`. S3.4 candidates: a recommending buy/sell/hold form (imperatives in singular and plural such as `kup(cie)`, `kupuj(cie)`, `sprzedaj(cie)`, `trzymaj(cie)`, `zainwestuj w`, plus `kupno`, `sprzedaż`, `rekomendujemy`, `rekomenduję`, `polecam kupić`, `polecamy akcje …`, `warto kupić`, `co kupić`, `co sprzedać`; a descriptive present tense like `kupują` or a first-person past like `kupiłam` does not count) within ±1 sentence of an instrument noun from Art. 2-3 (`akcje` but not `akcja promocyjna`, `obligacje`, `ETF`, `CFD`, plural `opcje` but not `opcja roczna`, `kontrakty terminowe`, `futures`, `certyfikaty inwestycyjne`, `jednostki funduszy`), negated hits excluded up to the next comma (`nie udzielamy rekomendacji`, `nie polecamy kupna`). Attribution (decision 9) checks every candidate sentence, not only the quoted one. S3.5: `abonament`, `subskrypcja`, `sygnały premium`, `VIP`, `płatna grupa`, a monthly price. S3.6/S1.7: `zł`/`PLN` amounts, `+48` numbers, a `.pl` page host, else the `pl` language guess with null evidence. S3.8: the S3.4 verbs within ±1 sentence of a plain crypto-asset word, with no Art. 2 instrument nearby.

No ADR: these choices are local to `analytics/`, follow the accepted plan, the challenge criteria and ADRs 0301, 0304 and 0305, and are reversible by editing YAML or one module. ADR 0308 stays unused.

## Risks / Trade-offs

- [Rules-only M3 cannot tell education from advice on hard cases] → the gate caps pages without an S3.4 candidate; candidates are `uncertain` ("do weryfikacji"); the LLM refines them when `analytics-llm-p0` lands.
- [Sentence-level attribution (decision 9) misattributes an offer, e.g. a finfluencer names the firm in the same sentence as their own paid group] → the M1 finding is emitted either way; the S3.3/S3.4 LLM questions ask whose offer it is; listed as an assumption to confirm.
- [The Polish-targeting filter drops a foreign firm's ad written in English but aimed at Poland without PLN, `+48` or `.pl`] → rare for ads aimed at Polish retail clients; the reason `not_targeting_poland` is logged with the document id, so dropped documents can be counted.
- [Handle-based publisher identity: a fan account named `@alfa_inwestycje_fan` is not the firm, but `@alfainwestycje` by a squatter would be routed to M2] → exact normalized equality only; profile host and page host win first; noted for the real KNF loader.
- [Short dictionary words (`VIP`, `partner`, `dołącz`) cause false signals] → word-boundary regexes, negative test cases per signal, small weights compared with S1.2/S1.3 and S3.4.
- [Metadata evidence weakens the "quote from the text" rule] → the quote must still be a verbatim substring of a declared field of the same document; LLM verdicts are checked against `text` only.
- [Hand-tuned weights drift from real data] → weights live in YAML; tests assert orderings and threshold sides; calibration is plan §8.
- [Seam drift before `analytics-llm-p0` freezes its interface] → this change uses only the YAML `llm:` block, `Rule.ask_llm` and the `judge` keyword; group 6 re-reads `llm/judge.py` first.
- [Both stage-2 changes modify "M2 finding assembly" in `document-analysis` and edit `rules/engine.py` and `pipeline.py`] → different scenarios and additive code; the second to archive merges the requirement text against the main spec.
- [A re-scan moves a document between modules (a firm added to the registry turns M3 into M1)] → the worker upserts per `(document_id, module)`, so the old finding stays; acceptable for the hackathon.

## Migration Plan

Pure code change in `analytics/`; no table, contract or event change. Rollback is reverting the branch: `third_party`/`unknown` documents go back to `module_not_implemented`, unmatched `firm_own` pages back to M2. Findings already stored stay valid v1 rows.

## Resolved Questions

The product rules were answered by Damian through the coordinating session (challenge criteria, 2026-11-21) and are built into the decisions above. The three assumptions this change added were then decided by the coordinator:

1. An advisory offer is attributed to the registered firm only when the sentence holding its evidence names the firm; otherwise it is the publisher's (decision 9). Accepted as our heuristic: the challenge documents go no deeper, and the rule is explainable to an analyst.
2. The Polish-marker filter applies only to foreign firms (`foreign_branch`, `foreign_no_branch`), following "polskojęzyczne reklamy zagranicznych firm"; firms registered in Poland stay in M1 scope whatever the language, with S1.7 as an attribute (decision 5).
3. Crypto-only buy/sell calls are reported as S3.8 "poza art. 2", at most 0.4 and never lifting the gate (decision 11).
